Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG): An Overview

Retrieval-Augmented Generation, or RAG, is an architecture pattern that combines
information retrieval with text generation. Instead of relying solely on knowledge
baked into a language model's parameters during training, a RAG system retrieves
relevant information from an external knowledge base at query time and provides
that information to the model as context.

Why RAG matters

Large language models have two well-known limitations. First, their knowledge is
frozen at the point their training data was collected, so they cannot know about
recent events or documents that did not exist during training. Second, they can
hallucinate, producing plausible-sounding but factually incorrect statements,
especially when asked about specific facts, figures, or niche topics.

RAG addresses both limitations. By retrieving relevant passages from a trusted
document collection and feeding them into the model's context window alongside
the user's question, the model can ground its answer in real, verifiable source
material rather than relying purely on memorized patterns.

How a RAG pipeline works

A typical RAG pipeline has two phases. During the ingestion phase, documents are
loaded, split into smaller chunks, converted into numerical vector representations
called embeddings, and stored in a vector database. During the query phase, the
user's question is also converted into an embedding, and the vector database is
searched for the chunks whose embeddings are most similar to the question's
embedding. Those chunks are inserted into a prompt template along with the
original question, and the combined prompt is sent to a language model, which
generates a final answer grounded in the retrieved context.

Common components

Vector databases such as FAISS and Chroma are popular choices for storing and
searching embeddings efficiently. Embedding models convert text into fixed-length
numerical vectors that capture semantic meaning, so that similar concepts end up
close together in vector space. Orchestration frameworks such as LangChain and
LlamaIndex provide reusable building blocks for constructing these pipelines,
handling document loading, chunking, retrieval, and prompt construction.

Applications

RAG is widely used for building chatbots that can answer questions about internal
company documents, customer support systems that reference product manuals, legal
and financial research tools that search through contracts or filings, and
academic research assistants that search through papers. Because RAG systems cite
the specific source material used to generate an answer, they are generally
considered more trustworthy and easier to audit than models that answer purely
from memory.
